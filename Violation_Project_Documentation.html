Fraud Handling Process Flow

End-to-end process covering how a fraud case is submitted, triaged by offer profile, investigated through proof submission, and enforced with the appropriate controls. The process applies to both S1 (advertiser-reported) and S2 (internally detected) cases.

Two Delivery Phases Phase 1 is the current process using Mini Tools for case submission and handling — stakeholders receive cards to execute manually. Phase 2 is the automated state where Mini Tools applies all controls automatically and notifies stakeholders — no manual card creation needed.
How Offer Profile Fits Into the Flow All cases — both S1 (advertiser-reported) and S2 (internally detected) — follow the same proof submission step. The publisher is given a 5-working-day window to submit evidence of legitimate traffic. The Offer Profile (Growth-Focused, Balanced, or Protection-Focused) is recorded at ticket creation but is only consulted after proof fails or times out. It has no effect on the proof requirement itself.

On proof failure, enforcement is determined as follows:
  • Growth-Focused: Light treatment — publisher notified, case logged, traffic placed under monitoring. No immediate suspension.
  • Balanced / Protection-Focused: Full enforcement controls applied per the controls table — traffic suspension, payout blocking, conversion rejection, and where warranted, publisher ban.
  • S1 cases (any Offer Profile): On proof failure, the enforcement tier is automatically escalated to Protection-Focused — the strictest controls apply regardless of what profile the offer was assigned. The Offer Profile diamond is bypassed for S1 failures.

Fraud Category Framework

Six fraud categories have been defined for the Involve Asia affiliate network. Each category has a clear definition and a set of detection metrics used to identify violations.

Category 01
Porn / Gambling

Affiliate drives conversions from adult, porn, or gambling/betting sites that violate the advertiser's Terms of Service.

Detection Logic
Referrer domain classification:
  • Check referrer URL against a porn/gambling domain blocklist
  • Flag if referrer domain matches known adult/gambling category
Category 02
Cookie Stuffing / Cookie Dropping

The affiliate fires the advertiser's tracking cookie onto a user's browser without the user having clicked or seen any promotional placement.

Metric 1 — Multi-Offer Click Pattern
  • Flag conversions where multiple clicks are recorded across different offers from the same publisher
  • Time window: within 10 seconds (±5s from click timestamp) for a single user
Metric 2 — Click Source Type
  • Check if the single click source originates from an IMG tag or iFrame
  • IMG/iFrame-triggered clicks indicate invisible, user-unaware cookie firing
⚠ Note: Both metrics should be combined to confirm Cookie Stuffing. Either metric alone may not be sufficient to flag a violation.
Category 03
Pop-Under / Pop-Up Traffic

Traffic generated by forced pop-up or pop-under windows on low-quality or pirated-content sites. Most programs explicitly prohibit pop traffic.

Detection Logic
Both conditions must be met:
  • Conversion Rate (CR) < 0.5%
  • Total clicks > 100,000 per month
  • Offer's promotional method filter: Pop up, Popunder & Tabunder only
Category 04 ⚠ Work in Progress
Illegal Direct Link

Affiliate funnels traffic straight to the advertiser's tracking URL, bypassing legitimate promotional placements.

Detection Logic
  • Identify click_ids with abnormally high conversion volume
  • Remove duplicate orders using item-level tracking (adv_sub1)
  • Threshold: > 20 unique Order IDs (adv_sub1) per single click_id
Category 05 ⚠ Work in Progress
Brand Bidding

Bidding on a brand's trademarked keywords in search engine advertising when the brand's affiliate programme explicitly prohibits this.

Detection Logic
  • Referrer URL domain is a search engine: google.com, bing.com, yahoo.com
  • Cross-check offer's approved promotional method — must not include SEM/Search
Category 06 ⚠ Work in Progress
Empty Referrer URL

No referrer URL is recorded in clicks or conversions — traffic source is completely unknown and untraceable.

Detection Logic
  • Referrer URL field is empty or null in both click and conversion records
  • ⚠ No automated detection rule currently defined for this category
Category 07
Click Spam — Click Flooding

A publisher sends a massive volume of low-quality or bot-generated clicks to an affiliate link, artificially inflating click counts and attempting to steal last-click attribution from legitimate publishers.

Signal 1 — Click Volume Spike
  • Baseline: rolling 7-day hourly average clicks per publisher per offer
  • > 3× baseline (same hour) → Suspicious
  • > 5× baseline (same hour) → High Risk
  • Absolute cap: > 800 clicks/min triggers regardless of baseline
Signal 2 — Conversion Rate Collapse
  • Minimum sample: 200+ clicks in the measurement window before CVR signal activates
  • CVR drop > 30% vs 7-day average → Suspicious
  • CVR drop > 50% vs 7-day average → High Risk
Signal 3 — IP Concentration
  • Measurement window: 24-hour rolling
  • Top 5 IPs > 35% of total publisher clicks → Suspicious
  • Top 5 IPs > 50% of total publisher clicks → Likely Bot
Signal 4 — Repeat Clicks
  • Same user/IP generates > 10 clicks within 3 minutes → flagged
  • Constant interval pattern (clicks evenly spaced ± 100ms) → bot signal
Signal 5 — Traffic Quality
  • Single user-agent string > 40% of total clicks → automation indicator
  • Data-centre or VPN IP detected → quality flag
  • Referrer header missing or invalid → low-quality traffic signal
Signal 6 — Click-to-Conversion Time (CTIT)
  • CTIT < 10 seconds → click injection indicator (outside scope but flagged)
  • CTIT distribution abnormally uniform across > 50 clicks → automation signal
Signal 7 — Geographic / Device Mismatch
  • Click country ≠ conversion country for > 60% of sessions → suspicious
  • Mobile user-agent with desktop fingerprint → spoofing indicator
Fraud Scoring Engine: Signals are not binary triggers — each contributes to a cumulative risk score. Action is taken based on score bracket, not individual signal breach. See the Category 07 deep-dive section for full scoring table and response tiers.

SOP — Porn & Gambling Domain Blocking

Standard Operating Procedure for detecting and actioning affiliates that drive traffic from adult, porn, or gambling/betting sites that violate advertiser Terms of Service.

Scope This SOP applies to all violations where referrer domain analysis indicates traffic originating from adult, pornographic, or gambling/betting sites. It covers both advertiser-reported cases (S1) and internally detected cases (S2).
Mini Tools — Domain Blacklist (Module C) All domain blocking actions described in this SOP are managed through Mini Tools' Domain Blacklist module. Compliance submits the domain list to Kaishen via Mini Tools — not by direct email or manual tech ticket. For the weekly S2 cycle, Mini Tools pulls new candidates from Mahadir, cross-checks against the Trip.com advertiser-protected list, and generates the weekly change request (domains to remove + domains to add) for Kaishen to execute.

Category 07 — Click Spam (Click Flooding) Deep-Dive

Click Flooding is a form of click spam where a publisher sends a massive volume of low-quality or automated clicks to an affiliate link, artificially inflating their click count and attempting to claim last-click attribution from legitimate publishers. This section covers the full detection framework, fraud scoring engine, and enforcement tiers.

What is Click Flooding?

In an affiliate network, commission is typically awarded to the publisher whose tracking link was clicked last before a conversion occurs. Click flooding exploits this model — a fraudulent publisher fires thousands of clicks per day against multiple offers, betting that some legitimate users will happen to convert after one of their clicks gets recorded, even though the publisher played no real role in driving that conversion. The result: legitimate publishers are robbed of their commission, advertiser budgets are wasted, and network data quality degrades.

Click Flooding is distinct from Click Injection (an Android-specific exploit where clicks are fired at install time). This framework covers Click Flooding only — high-volume indiscriminate click spam across web-based affiliate offers.

Key principle — Scoring, not binary blocking: No single signal is sufficient to confirm click spam. A legitimate publisher running a viral campaign or a flash-sale promotion can temporarily spike 3–5× their baseline. The scoring engine accumulates evidence across multiple independent signals before escalating to action.

Detection Signals

S1 — Click Volume Spike

Baseline = rolling 7-day hourly average for the same publisher × offer combination. Measured at the same hour-of-day to account for natural traffic rhythms (e.g. peak hours).

  • Current hour > 3× 7-day hourly average → +20 pts
  • Current hour > 5× 7-day hourly average → +30 pts (replaces +20)
  • Absolute: > 800 clicks/min in any 1-minute window → +30 pts
S2 — Conversion Rate Collapse

CVR = conversions ÷ clicks over a 24-hour rolling window. Minimum 200 clicks required in the window before this signal activates — low-volume publishers are excluded to avoid false positives from statistical noise.

  • CVR drop > 30% vs 7-day average → +20 pts
  • CVR drop > 50% vs 7-day average → +30 pts (replaces +20)
S3 — IP Concentration

Measures what share of total publisher clicks originate from a small number of IP addresses over a 24-hour rolling window.

  • Top 5 IPs account for > 35% of clicks → +20 pts
  • Top 5 IPs account for > 50% of clicks → +30 pts (replaces +20)
S4 — Repeat Clicks & Interval Pattern

Detects rapid-fire clicking from a single source and bot-like timing regularity.

  • Same user/IP: > 10 clicks within any 3-minute window → +20 pts
  • Constant interval pattern: clicks evenly spaced ±100ms across ≥ 20 consecutive clicks → +30 pts
S5 — Traffic Quality Signals

Low-quality traffic indicators measured over a 24-hour rolling window.

  • Single user-agent > 40% of publisher clicks → +15 pts
  • Data-centre or VPN IP detected → +25 pts
  • Referrer header missing or invalid → +10 pts

Fraud Scoring Engine

All active signals are summed into a single cumulative risk score. Scores stack — a publisher triggering multiple signals will accumulate points from each. There is no maximum cap; scores above 90 all fall into the Critical tier.

Operational Notes

Baseline Stability Rule

The 7-day rolling hourly baseline only updates during periods when the publisher's score is below 30. This prevents a fraudulent traffic ramp from inflating the baseline and masking future spikes.

Score Reset

Risk score is recalculated every 24 hours. A publisher's score can decrease if signals drop below thresholds. Moving from Critical back to High Risk requires a compliance analyst to manually confirm before throttle enforcement is lifted.

Severe Signal Override

If S4 (constant interval pattern) or S1 (absolute > 800 clicks/min) triggers, the publisher is immediately escalated to Suspicious (50+) regardless of total score. These signals have a very low false-positive rate and warrant immediate attention.

Minimum Volume Gates

S2 (CVR) requires ≥ 200 clicks in window. Signals that don't meet their minimum gate contribute 0 points until the gate is reached.

Out of Scope — Click Injection: Click Injection is an Android-specific exploit where malicious apps listen for app-install broadcast events and fire a click immediately before the install completes, stealing attribution credit. While related, it requires a separate detection framework using CTIT distribution analysis on a per-app basis. Click Injection is not covered under Category 07 and is earmarked for a future category (Category 08 TBC).
